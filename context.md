**Isobar interval: 1 hPa, stated in the panel.** Mid-latitude synoptic charts run 4 hPa. Measured on the live payload, this box's sea-level pressure spreads 4.2–7.8 hPa *within a single hour* (mean 5.95 over 48 hours), so a 4 hPa step draws one contour or none. **Known visual characteristic: the isobars are angular, and that is the grid, not the interval.** The perturbation test proves the 1 hPa lines are *stable*; it cannot prove they are *smooth*, and they are not. The synoptic grid is 5×5 nodes. Measured on the live hour `2026-10-17T03:00Z` (spread 5.90 hPa), 1 hPa yields 6 levels / 6 lines of which **3 are closed loops with only 3–7 vertices** — small polygons hugging individual cells, which is what reads as rectangles and boxes on the map. At 4 hPa there is 1 such loop of 5 vertices; at 2 hPa, 1 of 3–6. So the finer interval makes the artefact more visible (3 loops instead of 1) but does not create it. Smoothing the isolines would draw curvature the 174 km grid does not resolve, and dropping small loops would hide real structure; both would trade honesty for looks. The honest fix is a denser grid, which is a provider change, not a rendering one. Left as-is and recorded here so the next reader does not mistake it for a new bug — or try to "improve" it.

Two of the compass guards also failed to bite at first: one matched the function signature rather than the call site, and another proved the label interpolated `${tail}` without proving `tail` came from the valid time. ## 29 October 2026 · Blind-spot repair, the source status, and what the receipt could not see

The thin top bar that shipped on 28 October stood in front of the masthead as a plain `<div>`. The spatial instrument counts a band that is fixed, sticky, or an in-flow `<header>` outside `<main>`; a `<div>` is none of those, and by standing in front of the masthead it pushed the masthead off `top:0`, so the masthead stopped being a band too. The instrument's reading fell from 23px of top chrome to 0px, the receipt was re-baselined to 0px, and the gate went green on a page that has chrome. Zero is smaller than 23, and a smaller number reads as an improvement.

The bar also carried a second clock (the footer had one), an inline script (so its hard-coded Indonesian could not be translated on a bilingual page, and could not be tested), an `aria-live` region around a clock that ticked every second (a screen reader asked to announce the time once a second), and a "N umpan langsung" count that measured DOM elements with text — a feed showing an error message counted as live. All of it was visible in the pixels; none of it was in the receipt.

Two repairs, both permanent rather than cosmetic:

1. **The chrome census** (`scripts/chrome-census.mjs`, `npm run audit:chrome`). The receipt and the page now answer for each other: the receipt says what the chrome is, the census measures independently what is painted above the content, and if they disagree the receipt is wrong. Content inside `<main>` is content — the census counts what the instrument counts, plus what it cannot see. It runs in the release before the stamp, and it carries a falsifier (`npm run audit:chrome:falsify`) that plants the exact band shape that shipped and requires the census to catch it. A gate that has never failed protects nothing. Full receipt `docs/CHROME-CENSUS.md`.

2. **The source status** — the honest replacement for the bar, and the gap the daily-monitoring pilot names: one line per source the page reads at runtime, with the publisher, the tier the publisher stamped, the stamped time, its age, and whether it is inside the freshness rule the product already applies to that kind of source. The rules live once, in `SOURCE_RULES`, and the panels read the same numbers — a status view that disagreed with the panel it describes would be a second opinion, and a second opinion is not evidence. A refused source is a refusal (never a zero, never a silence, never a stale reading); a source that did not answer is named as such. It is bilingual, it announces through a status region rather than on a timer, and it adds no band: it lives inside `<main>`, where the instrument can see the masthead again.

The strip changed the phone's arithmetic, and that had to be paid for honestly: the map is sized to leave room for what stands above it, so the footer and the last of the map still stand on one screen.

One correction worth recording, because it nearly became a wrong fix. The phone renders the observations and warnings panels at zero height, and the first reading of that was "the content is unreachable". It is not: `dockPanels()` moves the readings into the Cuaca dialog and the warnings into the warnings dialog below the console width, by design, and the panels are there — closed. A CSS change made on that false premise collapsed the phone map from 785px to 170px; it is reverted, the map stands at 756px, and the phone shows the source status, the map, and the footer on one screen.
